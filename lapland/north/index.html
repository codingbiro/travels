How it works

Loose on purpose: a few anchors a day, the rest decided by the sky.

Four rules

  1. Monday and Tuesday swap freely. Put Siida on the greyer day.
  2. Sunday stays outdoors. Siida and Laanilan Kievari are closed on Sundays.
  3. Every evening: 18:00 cloud check, then sauna. Each cool-off is a sky check.
  4. Nothing beyond an hour from base. The one or two exceptions are flagged.

The ritual

  1. 15:30

    Is anything coming?

    FMI's auroras and space weather page. Its magnetometers include Ivalo and Kevo, right on your doorstep — a sharp jump there means go outside.

  2. 18:00

    Where's the hole?

    Yr's cloud map and Windy's low, mid and high cloud layers. Clear overhead: stay home. Clouded in but clear within 45 min: go.

  3. 19:00–01:00

    Watch in spells

    Out every 15–20 min between sauna rounds; most displays fall between about 21:00 and 01:00. It often starts as a faint grey arc low in the north that the camera shows green before your eyes do.

Photos

Daylight with the 70–350, darkness with the 12 mm.

The sun peaks at about 7° around noon, due south, and sets in the south-west. Every clear hour is low, raking light — and there are only about six and a half of them. On the a6100 the 70–350 frames like a 105–525 mm lens, and since the body has no in-body stabilisation, the lens's OSS does all the steadying.

Daylight: a6100 + 70–350 G

Mode
A or M with Auto ISO, capped at 6400. At 350 mm the lens is f/6.3 and November light is dim, so let ISO rise rather than shutter speed fall.
Shutter
1/250 for landscapes, 1/500 for people and standing reindeer, 1/1000–1/2000 for running huskies and jays in flight.
Focus
AF‑C with Zone or Tracking; Animal Eye AF for the dogs. Burst on Hi+.

Night: a6100 + Samyang 12 mm f/2

Mode
M. Top dial is aperture, rear wheel is shutter.
Exposure
f/2, ISO 1600–3200. 3–6 s for bright, fast curtains; 8–15 s for a faint arc. Stars stay sharp to about 15 s at 12 mm.
Focus
The lens has no AF/MF switch: set MF in the menu, magnify on a bright star, turn until it's smallest. It focuses by wire, so re-check after the camera sleeps or you swap batteries.
File

Tripod

Where the 70–350 earns its place

  • Tue 12:00–15:13
    Kaunispää tower. Stack the layered fells of Urho Kekkonen National Park at 200–350 mm with the low sun behind them. The sun stays in the south all day and sets south-west.
  • Tue 10:00
    Husky & Co. Get down to the dogs' eye level, 1/1000 or faster. Ask before photographing the guides.
  • Any forest stop
    Siberian jays. Sit still with the lens ready; they swoop in fast, so 1/2000.
  • Roadsides, Mon farm
    Reindeer. 350 mm lets you stay far enough back not to move them. Shoot from the car only in a proper lay-by, never stopped on the road; at the farm, ask your hosts first.
  • Sun or Mon, Inari
    Juutua rapids. If ice is forming at the edges: tripod, 1/4–1 s at 70–150 mm for smooth water, or 1/1000 to freeze the spray.
  • Sun, Kiilopää
    Summit ridges. Distant fells with hikers for scale at 150–250 mm; the phone or the 12 mm for the wide view.
  • Around 15:15 daily
    Lake Inari shore. Pastel layers after sunset; the blue hour lasts about an hour.
  • Mon 22:37
    Bonus: moonrise. The moon clears the north-east horizon behind the fells. 350 mm, tripod; start at 1/250 s, f/6.3, ISO 400.

Cold-weather craft

  • NP‑FW50 batteries drain fast in the cold, and OSS plus continuous AF use more. Carry three or more in an inside pocket and rotate.
  • Before going indoors, seal the camera in a zip bag and let it warm up before opening it, so no condensation forms inside.
  • Lens hood on all day — at 7° the sun is always close to the frame.
  • Lens cap on between shots in snowfall; microfibre cloth in the chest pocket.

Sámi homeland

Everywhere on this trip is inside the Sámi homeland, and Inari is its cultural centre in Finland.

MUST

Siida

The Sámi Museum, European Museum of the Year 2024, and since 2025 run by the Sámi Museum alone. Its main exhibition links Sámi culture with northern nature. Monday.

MUST

A herding family

Angeli, Renniina or Inari Reindeer Farm (see Monday). This is where questions get real answers — earmarks, round-ups, a year with reindeer.

FILL

Sajos

The Sámi cultural centre in Inari village, next to Siida. Its Duodji Shop sells Sámi-made handicraft.

IF

Pielpajärvi church

Built 1752–60 at the old Inari Sámi winter village. Open to anyone who walks in, free.

FILL

Nellim

Finns, Inari Sámi and Skolt Sámi side by side on Lake Inari's south-east shore, with a wooden Skolt Orthodox church. 42 km past Ivalo — just over your cap.

Three languages

Inari Sámi, North Sámi and Skolt Sámi all live here; Siida's website runs in all three. Welcome, in Inari Sámi: tiervâpuáttim.

Being a good guest

  • Ask before photographing people, homes or herders at work.
  • Buy handicraft with the Sámi Duodji label — it guarantees a Sámi maker.
  • Skip costumes and souvenirs that imitate the gákti, the Sámi dress.
  • Every reindeer has an owner. Don't chase, feed or call free-roaming reindeer.

Food

One local-food day out, the rest cooked at home.

Local-food day: Monday

  • Lunch at Sarrit, Siida. Buffet 11–15: warm main, salad table, in-house baking, dessert, coffee.
  • Dinner at Laanilan Kievari, 2 km south of Saariselkä. Mon–Sat 16–22; book. Lappish ingredients with French and Karelian touches; order the fire-roasted reindeer shoulder for two.
  • Laanilan Kievari is about an hour from Inari, so take an early table on Tuesday's Saariselkä day, or cook Lappish at home on Monday.
  • Not this trip: Aanaar at Wilderness Hotel Juutua in Inari is closed for renovation 15 Oct – 22 Nov 2026.

Home menu

  • Poronkäristys. Frozen shaved reindeer fried in butter with onion, then simmered 30–40 min. Buttery mash, lingonberries, pickled cucumber.
  • Lohikeitto. Salmon soup with potato, leek, cream and dill; rye bread on the side.
  • Whitefish or Arctic char (siika, nieriä), pan-fried in butter — if the Ivalo counter has it, buy it.
  • Leipäjuusto. Squeaky baked cheese, warmed in a pan, with cloudberry jam.
  • Breakfast. Karelian pies with egg butter, porridge with lingonberries, rye bread.
  • Trail lunch. Sausages for the campfire, mustard, a thermos of coffee and cocoa.

Sauna

Every night, at home. Each cool-off doubles as a sky check.

  1. Heat. Switch on 45–60 min ahead; aim for 70–80 °C.
  2. Löyly. A ladle of water on the stones, rounds of 10–15 min. Lower bench for kids and first-timers.
  3. Cool off outside. Towel, boots, look north. Roll in the snow if there's any.
  4. Repeat two or three times. Water between rounds; nothing stronger if you might still drive to an aurora spot.

Wildcards

  • FILLKuurakaltio smoke sauna, Kiilopää. A traditional smoke sauna beside a fell stream with a cold plunge. Public winter sessions are listed Monday to Saturday, but the fell centre runs reduced services before mid-November — confirm first.
  • FILLLaanilan Kievari's saunas. Two saunas, an outdoor hot tub and a cold dip, by reservation — pairs with the Monday dinner.

Driving

  • Winter tyres are compulsory in Finland from December to February and whenever conditions call for them — in Lapland in November, they will. Confirm your rental has them; studded is best.
  • Reindeer stand on the road and don't move for cars. Slow to walking pace and pass wide. If you hit one, call 112 so the police can tell the owner.
  • Many cabins have a car-heating socket; plug in on nights below about −10 °C.
  • Aurora drives stay short on purpose, with one sober driver every night.

Calendar traps

  • Sat 31 Oct is All Saints' Day, a public holiday: check shop hours for your arrival evening.
  • Sundays: Siida and Laanilan Kievari are closed.
  • Aanaar is closed until 22 November.
  • Kiilopää parking is paid 9–18 via EasyPark.

Weather and safety

  • November averages about −3 °C by day and −8 °C at night and is mostly overcast: cloud, not aurora, is the real gamble.
  • Snow is likely but not guaranteed in early November.
  • New lake ice is not safe. Stay off frozen lakes and rivers, however solid they look.
  • Parts of the national park have no mobile signal. Emergency number: 112.
